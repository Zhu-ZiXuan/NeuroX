# Parallel BL/SL DC solver

The DC solve is one package of stateless, topology-agnostic software: the `Solver` base with config-keyed registry dispatch and the shared `SolverConfig` / `SolverDcop` / `SolverResiduals` containers, the concrete `NestedParallelRailSolver`, the shared batched (block-)tridiagonal linear-algebra solves and wire-KCL builders, and the structural `ClampDriver` Protocol naming the boundary-clamp surface the solve consumes. The nested Newton formulation, its well-posedness, and the parallel-rail structural assumptions belong to the Reference spec; this document covers the software design and trade-offs.

## Design decisions

- **The solver is stateless and topology-agnostic; the cell and both clamp drivers are per-call parameters of `solve_dc`.** `Solver.__init__` takes only its config; it binds no boundary actors. `solve_dc` receives the cell, the BL driver, and the SL driver (plus their snaps) per call, so one solver serves any SL/BL topology whose cell condenses to a two-terminal branch. The solver calls `cell.solve_branch(v_bl, v_sl, cell_snap)` at each wire-Newton step and assembles the block-$2\times2$ wire Jacobian from the returned single current and two signed conductances, and calls each driver's transfer law at each outer step; it stamps no device current, models no internal cell node, and hard-wires no specific driver.
- **The class is non-generic; `solve_dc` is method-generic.** The cell / driver types vary per call, not per construction, so the type parameters live on the method, not the class. `solve_dc` is generic over `(CellSnapT, CellDCOPT, BLSnapT, SLSnapT)` — `CellSnapT` / `CellDCOPT` carry the cell's own bounds (`XbarCellSnap` / `XbarCellDcop`, mirroring `XbarCell`), the two driver snaps are bound to `ClampSnap`, matching `ClampDriver`. `SolverDcop` is `Generic[CellDCOPT]`, so `solve_dc` returns `SolverDcop[CellDCOPT]` and the concrete cell DCOP type flows through to the caller end to end (a concrete cell call yields `SolverDcop[<that cell's DCOP>]`, not `SolverDcop[Any]`). Keeping the parameters on the method, not the class, is what avoids a `**kwargs` boundary-handle signature while staying fully type-checked.
- **Solvers are stateless tool classes, not `nn.Module`s.** They carry no buffers and no registered state, so they stay out of the core's module tree — otherwise the fabricate cascade and `state_dict` would wrongly sweep them in. The cell holds the device state; the solver holds the method (see [cell](cell/README.md)).
- **Registry keyed on the config type.** A concrete solver registers against its `SolverConfig` subclass via `@Solver.register_key(SomeSolverConfig)`; callers reach it through `Solver.from_config(config=..., series_axis=...)`, which takes the config and the layout series-axis; the boundary actors are per-call arguments of `solve_dc`, not construction-time fields.
- **`series_axis` is a construction-time int, not a config field.** Of the two trailing cell-grid axes the solver canonicalises series-last (`[..., line, series]`); the consuming caller passes its layout's `series_axis` to `Solver.from_config` (e.g. `-1` for a series-last layout), stored as `self._series_axis`. It is deliberately *not* in `SolverConfig` / TOML — it is a structural property of the consuming array layout, not a calibrated numerical knob. At the entry of `solve_dc` / `solve_array_fixed_clamp`, `series_axis == -1` is a byte-identical no-op fast path; `series_axis == -2` swaps the two trailing cell-grid axes to canonical on the way in and restores the caller's layout on the way out (per-line driver snaps and clamp outputs stay put). Because `_series_axis` is a plain Python int it is a compile-time constant under `@torch.compile(dynamic=False)` — the branch is specialised away, so the `-1` graph is exactly the canonical body with no permute and no recompile.
- **Solvers have no Policy.** Every knob is either a workload-tuned numerical constant (`SolverConfig` subclass) or a method-intrinsic safety bound (`ClassVar` on the solver), neither of which is a per-source non-ideality toggle. So the consuming caller's policy carries no `solver` field.
- **Decompose the solve (nested) rather than one simultaneous Newton over all unknowns.** A simultaneous Newton over every wire / clamp unknown admits rail pseudo-equilibria and can leave a uA-scale BL wire residual; the nested block-Gauss-Seidel split into well-posed sub-problems eliminates both.
- **Iteration counts come from `solver_calibrate`, not hand-tuning** (chip-parameter-free step-ratio plateau; see [calibration guide](../../../guides/calibration/README.md)). The picker emits the raw plateau plus a fixed $+1$ outer margin; the resulting `n_outer` / `n_inner` are stored in the chip config and that is their only authoritative home. The per-cell condensation count `n_newton` is calibrated separately and owned by the cell (see [cell](cell/README.md)).
- **Shared residual / driver-current builders for consistent diagnostics.** `col_wire_kcl_residual` and `col_driver_current` are the single source for per-column wire-ladder KCL and boundary current. Sharing them keeps residual diagnostics numerically identical across every solver that builds the same wire ladder, rather than each solver re-deriving the stamping.
- **Numerical solves accumulate into lists, never in-place.** Both Thomas sweeps (`solve_tridiagonal`) and the block-Thomas sweeps (`solve_block_tridiagonal`) accumulate intermediate results to stay compile-friendly. In-place tensor writes break `torch.compile` graph tracing; pure-functional accumulation lets inductor fuse the per-step elementwise ops across the unrolled loop.
- **Fixed shape convention for the block solver (no `dim` arg).** `solve_block_tridiagonal` fixes the $N$ axis as third-to-last for the block tensors (`[..., N, B, B]`) and second-to-last for the RHS (`[..., N, B]`); callers transpose at the call site if their layout differs. The scalar `solve_tridiagonal` instead takes an explicit `dim` because its slices are rank-uniform with the RHS and an axis argument costs nothing.
- **One block solve per step, not two.** The block forward sweep stacks the super-diagonal block and the RHS column into a single right-hand side and issues one `torch.linalg.solve` per row instead of two, then splits the result back into the coupling factor $C_k$ and the reduced RHS $d_k$.
- **Method-intrinsic damping caps are `ClassVar`s, not config.** They are per-iteration $|\Delta u|$ damping bounds (a Newton heuristic, not a chip parameter), so they never enter the calibrated config or any Policy. `NestedParallelRailSolver` carries an outer cap `MAX_OUTER_STEP__V` (applied to the coupled $2\times2$ clamp step) and an inner cap `MAX_INNER_STEP__V` (applied to the block-$2\times2$ wire step).

### Nested solver structure

The nested formulation is the Reference spec; the code realizes it in three phases. A **warm start** seeds the IR-drop wire voltages and the two boundary clamps. An **outer loop** of `n_outer` steps then runs clamp-first: each step takes one outer clamp-Newton step, then `n_inner` inner wire-Newton steps at the updated clamps, so the loop exits with the wire state already converged for the latest clamp and needs no post-loop consistency step. Every inner step calls `cell.solve_branch` for the branch current and signed conductances; the coupled block handles a variable-SL clamp through the same code path, with no special case. Both Newtons are damped by per-iteration $|\Delta u|$ caps. An **exit refresh** calls `cell.solve_dc` to re-align the returned cell DCOP and internal node with the final wire voltages, which the last inner step leaves stale.

The solve carries two kinds of scalar — calibrated iteration counts (`NestedParallelRailSolverConfig`, loaded from the chip config) and method-intrinsic damping caps (`ClassVar`s on the solver):

| Knob | Kind | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `n_outer` | config count | coupled outer Newton steps on $(V_{\mathrm{BL,CL}}, V_{\mathrm{SL,CL}})$ |
| `n_inner` | config count | inner block-Newton steps on the wire system per outer step |
| `MAX_OUTER_STEP__V` | ClassVar cap | outer-step $\lvert\Delta u\rvert$ damping cap |
| `MAX_INNER_STEP__V` | ClassVar cap | inner-step $\lvert\Delta u\rvert$ damping cap |

### The `ClampDriver` role

The boundary clamp the solver consumes is a structural `ClampDriver` Protocol (`clamp.py`) naming the two-member surface the array solve needs from any clamp — `snapshot(*, v_ref__V, shape, multi_coords) -> SnapT` (the reference clamp voltage is injected per call and rides in the returned snap, a `ClampSnap`) and `solve_clamp(i_port__uA, snap, *, v_clamp_init__V) -> (v_clamp__V, dVclamp_dI__MOhm)`. It is a role (a capability), not a kind (a registry base), and it lives in the solver package beside its only consumer; the concrete clamps in `neurox/primitive/analog` (`OpAmpTia`, `VoltageDriver`) satisfy it structurally without importing it.

- **A role, not a base class.** A clamp driver is a *horizontal* capability the solve needs from a boundary, not a place in any class tree. Each conforming circuit already lives in its own analog circuit family answering "what kind of circuit is this and how is it built" — the BL clamp is a `Tia` topology (its own registry and `from_config`), the SL clamp is a `VoltageDriver`. A shared `ClampDriver` *base class* would cut across those families, forcing a second inheritance axis and a least-common-denominator config onto circuits that are physically unlike and built differently. The Protocol instead captures only the thin surface they already share by shape, so a clamp conforms by duck-typing — no inheritance, no `RegistryMixin`, no config — and another CSA satisfies it the same way. A clamp's richer concrete handle (a TIA's own `solve_dc`) stays on the concrete class and is simply not named by the role.
- **Lives in the solver package, beside its only consumer.** The solve is the sole consumer of the surface, so the Protocol sits in `neurox/primitive/xbar/solver/` (DIP — the interface belongs to the consumer). `OpAmpTia` / `VoltageDriver` in `neurox/primitive/analog` do not import it; they conform by matching `snapshot` / `solve_clamp` exactly, parameter names and kinds included, which is what makes the duck-typing check pass.

## Contracts & invariants

- **The cell is the only device interface; the drivers are the only boundary interface.** The solver reads device behaviour exclusively through `cell.solve_branch` (hot path) and `cell.solve_dc` (final refresh), and boundary behaviour through each driver's clamp transfer, passing the snaps it received. It consumes the cell's condensed-branch and signed-conductance contract unchecked when assembling the wire Jacobian, so a cell that violates the signs silently corrupts the solve rather than raising.
- **`@torch.compile` constraints (whole solve).** No in-place tensor writes; no Python-side branches on tensor values; no autograd reads of attributes that change across calls. Respecting them yields a single fused kernel over the solve, including the numerical solves it calls.
- **Shared containers decouple the caller from the solver.** `SolverDcop` carries the wire / clamp state plus the condensed cell DCOP (`SolverDcop.cell`); `SolverResiduals` carries the wire / clamp KCL residuals, while the per-cell internal-KCL residual lives on the cell DCOP. The core's data interface does not change with the solver.
- **Residuals are opt-in.** They are computed only when `compute_residuals=True`; the hot path leaves them `None` so the extra evaluations are elided from the compiled graph.
- **Wire-ladder layout for the residual builders.** `col_wire_kcl_residual` and `col_driver_current` assume the wire runs along the row axis with the driver at the boundary. `segment_g` (shape `(row_num,)`) encodes the per-segment conductances along that wire; its ordering is boundary-dependent, with the driver segment at the boundary end and the cell-to-cell segments following toward the far end.
- **Sign / unit conventions.** The KCL residual is returned in uA with the convention $r = i_{\mathrm{inject}} + \Delta v_{\mathrm{left}} \cdot g_{\mathrm{left}} + \Delta v_{\mathrm{right}} \cdot g_{\mathrm{right}}$, where $\Delta v_{\mathrm{left}}[k] = v[k] - v[k-1]$ (and $v[0] - v_{\mathrm{drive}}$ at the driver end) and $\Delta v_{\mathrm{right}}[k] = v[k] - v[k+1]$ (zero at the far end, which has no right segment). Node voltages are V, segment conductances uS, currents uA.
- **Boundary slots are ignored, not validated.** In `solve_tridiagonal` the `sub` entry at index 0 and the `sup` entry at the last index are unused boundary slots; in `solve_block_tridiagonal` the `sub` block at $k=0$ and the `sup` block at $k=N-1$ are unused. The algorithms never read them — callers need not zero them.
- **Block-count semantics ($B$).** `solve_block_tridiagonal` works for any block size $B$ from the same code path: $B=1$ reduces to scalar Thomas and matches `solve_tridiagonal` numerically up to fp64 round-off, and $B=2$ is the coupled two-rail wire Newton the nested solver uses; larger $B$ runs through the identical sweep for any topology that couples more node classes per row. The single-block fast path ($N=1$) is one $B \times B$ `torch.linalg.solve`.
- **Elementwise derivative diagonal.** `elementwise_diff(fn, *, wrt, **kwargs)` returns the per-element derivative of an elementwise device function via one autograd backward pass. Because `fn` is elementwise, the derivative of the summed output with respect to each input element is that element's own derivative, so a single backward yields the full Jacobian diagonal without materialising off-diagonal terms.

## Performance & resources

- **Cell condensation sets the array memory.** Condensing the cell's internal node removes one unknown per cell from the array solve, so the wire Jacobian stays block-$2\times2$ (`[..., col, row, 2, 2]`) rather than carrying the internal node — the memory headroom that lets fp64 / large-batch workloads run. fp32 is the chip-preset default for speed and memory; fp64 is for accuracy verification. Chunking the leading batch is a caller-side handle, not the solver's, for when other GPU users compete.
- **Thomas vs block-Thomas cost.** Scalar Thomas is $O(N)$ elementwise work per system; block-Thomas is $O(N \cdot B^3)$ with one $B \times B$ solve per row, so memory stays $O(N \cdot B^2)$ per leading-batch instance. The block solver carries one extra trailing rank versus the scalar solver even at $B=1$.
- **Unrolled-loop fusion.** Because the sweeps build Python lists and `torch.stack` once, the per-step ops are pure-functional and inductor fuses the unrolled forward and back sweeps; there is no per-iteration kernel launch in the compiled graph.
- **No materialised dense matrix.** The Thomas path never assembles the full $N B \times N B$ system, so its per-instance memory does not scale with $N^2$.

## Gotchas

- **`B=1` block-Thomas is not bit-identical to scalar Thomas.** It agrees only up to fp64 round-off because the $1 \times 1$ `torch.linalg.solve` path differs arithmetically from the scalar division in `solve_tridiagonal`; the wire Newton uses $B=2$, but use the scalar helper when exact reproducibility against the scalar path matters.
- **`solve_block_tridiagonal` has no `dim` argument by design.** Passing data in a different axis order silently mis-indexes the block sweep rather than erroring; transpose to `[..., N, B, B]` / `[..., N, B]` before calling.
- **`segment_g` ordering is boundary-dependent — verify it matches the wire topology.** A mismatched ordering shifts every wire conductance and quietly corrupts the residual rather than erroring; `col_wire_kcl_residual` and `col_driver_current` share the same convention, so an inconsistency corrupts both.
- **Do not narrow `ClampDriver`'s `SnapT` bound below `ClampSnap`.** Narrowing it to any one clamp's snap base silently drops every clamp whose snap does not inherit that base (`OpAmpTiaSnap` and `VoltageDriverSnap` are unrelated dataclasses); the structural `ClampSnap` bound is what keeps the role open to unlike clamps.
- **Do not re-bind the solver to a concrete clamp.** Replacing the per-call `ClampDriver[SnapT]` parameters with stored `Tia` / `VoltageDriver` fields would reintroduce the second inheritance axis the role exists to avoid and recouple the solver to a clamp kind.

## Known limitations

- **No pivoting in the Thomas sweeps.** Both `solve_tridiagonal` and `solve_block_tridiagonal` assume the modified diagonal (block) stays non-singular through the forward sweep; they perform no row/block pivoting. Well-posedness is the responsibility of the Newton formulation, not these numerical solves.
- **Builders cover the column-oriented wire only.** `col_wire_kcl_residual` / `col_driver_current` are specialised to the `dim=-1` column wire; a row-oriented wire uses the sibling row-wire builders in the same module rather than a `dim` parameter on these.
- **Cell condensation is verified through residuals, not a global Jacobian.** With the single nested formulation, correctness rests on the converged-residual checks (wire / clamp residuals on the solver DCOP, per-cell internal-KCL residual on the cell DCOP) and the physical-vs-ideal agreement — not on cross-agreement with a second formulation. Those checks are the standing evidence.
